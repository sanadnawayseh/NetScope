        Exact ICMP RTT · ms

        RTT is the mean of successful echo replies in each five-probe cycle. Cycles containing replies reported as <1 ms retain an average upper bound instead of fabricated exact RTT statistics. Lines also break across collection gaps longer than five minutes. Gaps indicate failed, unavailable, bounded, or legacy measurements; historical process timings are preserved but excluded from RTT.

        NAME RESOLUTION

        DNS diagnostics

        Look up a public domain using this computer's system resolver.

        Enter a domain to begin. No lookup runs automatically.

        Display-only lookup: returned addresses are never contacted or added to monitoring. Local-only names are blocked, and non-public addresses are withheld. Up to 6 lookups per minute; one resolver job at a time. Lookup duration includes system resolver/cache work and is not ping latency.

        What do common DNS failures mean?

        Name or address not found: Check spelling. The name may not exist, or it may have no A/AAAA address records.

        Temporary failure or timeout: Your network or configured DNS server may be slow or unavailable. Try again later; this does not mean the website is down.

        No addresses: The resolver returned no usable IPv4 or IPv6 addresses. Other DNS record types may still exist.

        Restricted results: The name points to non-public addresses. NetScope withholds these to protect local resources.

        ROUTE DIAGNOSTICS

        Traceroute

        Manually trace a destination you own or are authorized to test. Windows only.

        No trace has run. Enter an authorized destination to begin.

        Loopback and RFC1918 private IPv4 literals only. No hostnames or public destinations. Up to 20 hops, 500 ms per probe, 35 seconds total, and 2 traces per minute; one trace at a time. This manual diagnostic does not run during monitoring or write history.

        Each hop shows three reported probe times. A * means that probe received no reply in time. Routers may filter or deprioritize trace replies; an intermediate timeout does not prove a router or network is down. The destination must actually respond before it is labeled reached.
